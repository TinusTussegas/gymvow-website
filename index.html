The evidence

Clause 02 / 04

This isn't a gimmick.
It's prospect theory.

In 1979, Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky showed that people don't weigh gains and losses equally: losing money hurts roughly twice as much as winning the same amount feels good. Four decades of research since, a Nobel Prize among it, keeps finding the same asymmetry.

Most fitness apps push rewards at the weak side of that equation. GymVow points the strong side at your gym habit.

Kahneman & Tversky

1979 · Econometrica

"Prospect Theory: An Analysis of Decision under Risk" was the paper that first showed losses loom larger than gains. It helped earn the 2002 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economics.

Tversky & Kahneman

1992 · J. Risk & Uncertainty

Put a number on the asymmetry: people weight a loss about 2.25 times as heavily as an equal gain.

Giné, Karlan & Zinman

2010 · AEJ: Applied

Smokers who voluntarily staked their own money on quitting were significantly more likely to pass nicotine tests, six and even twelve months later.

Royer, Stehr & Sydnor

2015 · AEJ: Applied

Employees who backed their gym attendance with a self-funded commitment contract kept showing up long after reward-only incentives wore off.

Charness & Gneezy

2009 · Econometrica

Getting people through the gym door repeatedly built habits that outlasted the incentives. Attendance stayed up after payments stopped.

A vow is pre-commitment: you bind your future self now, while you actually mean it. The card is on file. The rules run without asking. The version of you that wants to skip never gets a vote.

Questions

Clause 04 / 04

The fine print, out loud.

What happens if I complete my vow?

Nothing. Your card is never charged. GymVow doesn't hold your stake up front, so completing a vow costs $0. Money only ever moves when you miss.

What does one missed session cost?

Depends on your mode. Gradual: every miss forfeits 10% of your original stake, so ten misses drains it and fails the vow. Winner Takes All: the first miss forfeits everything, instantly.

When is my card actually charged?

When a week of your vow closes with fewer check-ins than your goal. The shortfall is charged automatically, so you're never asked to confirm your own penalty.

Do I need a payment method on file?

Yes. An active vow requires a valid payment method. If yours is missing or invalid, you get a warning and seven days to add one. If you don't, your vow is cancelled.

How does GymVow know I went to the gym?

Stay at your selected gym for at least 20 minutes. GymVow verifies the session from your location. No photos, no honor system.

How much do I have to stake?

$10 minimum, no maximum. Pick a number that would genuinely sting to lose. That sting is the product.

Who gets my money if I fail?

Depends on your plan. Free: failed stakes go to GymVow, after Stripe's payment-processing fees. Paid ($4.99/month): failed stakes are donated to Good Sports (goodsports.org) after Stripe's fees, and GymVow keeps nothing.

What's the difference between the free and paid plan?

Both have the full accountability mechanics. Free costs nothing monthly; if you fail, the stake goes to GymVow. Paid is $4.99/month; if you fail, the stake is donated to Good Sports. Stripe's processing fees are deducted either way.
