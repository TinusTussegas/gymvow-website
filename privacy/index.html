The short version

  • We collect your location only during a check-in. When you tap Check in, your phone sends a location reading every 30 seconds or so for 20 minutes. Then it stops. We do not track you between check-ins, ever.
  • We do not keep a trail of where you have been. Our server compares each reading to the gym and discards the coordinates. What we store is the gym, the times, and how many readings were out of range, not a map of your movements.
  • We never see your card number. Stripe handles it. We store the brand, the last four digits, the expiry, and the name on the card.
  • A computer decides whether your session counted and whether you are charged. That is the product working as designed, and section 5 explains your right to have a person look at it.
  • We do not sell your data, we do not share it for advertising, we do not profile you, and we do not use it to train artificial intelligence models. There are no advertising or analytics trackers in the app.
  • You can delete your account and everything in it, once your Vow has ended and nothing is owed. A small anonymized financial record survives, because Dutch law requires it.
  • GymVow is run from the Netherlands, so the GDPR applies to how we handle your data even though the app is offered only in the United States. That gives you more rights, not fewer.

This summary is not the policy. The sections below are.

2. Where your data comes from

Almost all of it comes from you, or from your device while you are using the app:

  • From you directly: your name, email, password, the Vows you configure, the gyms you save, and anything you write to us.
  • From your device, with your permission: location readings during a check-in, your push notification token, your time zone, and your IP address.
  • From our own systems: your check-in outcomes, your forfeitures, your charges, and an internal event log.
  • From Stripe: the brand, last four digits and expiry of your card, and the outcome of each charge.
  • From Google Maps: the names and locations of gyms near you.
  • From Apple: whether a Pro subscription is active, and nothing else.

We do not buy data, we do not receive data from data brokers, and we collect nothing about you from social networks or advertising networks.

A 20-minute check-in needs exactly that. We ask for the permission because the check-in needs it, not because we track you the rest of the time. We do not. Location is requested only between the moment you tap Check in and the moment that attempt ends.

If you withdraw location permission, nothing you have already done is affected, and we stop collecting location immediately. You will not be able to check in, and under section 7 of the Terms a Vow that is running will continue to cost you money. We are telling you that here so the choice is an informed one.

5. Automated decision-making

We are telling you this in plain terms because it matters more here than in most apps.

Whether a check-in counted, whether a session was missed, and whether money is forfeited and charged to your card are all decided automatically by our software, from your location readings and the calendar, with nobody looking at it. That decision has a real financial effect on you. It is automated decision-making within the meaning of GDPR Article 22.

Why we are allowed to do it. It is necessary for the performance of the contract between you and us, under Article 22(2)(a). The entire product is the promise that the rules run automatically, and that nobody, including us, can be talked out of them.

The logic involved. The rules are set out in full in sections 6 and 7 of the Terms of Service: the 20-minute window, the 100 metre radius plus accuracy tolerance, the two-consecutive-readings rule, the weekly goal, and the forfeiture rate you chose. Nothing is hidden, and nothing is weighted by anything about you personally.

Your safeguards, which are rights and not favours. You may: (a) obtain human intervention on any automated outcome; (b) express your point of view; and (c) contest the decision.

To use any of them, write to gymvow@outlook.com. A person will review the record of the attempt and reply. Section 6.6 of the Terms gives the same right in the contract with a 14-day window for check-in challenges, and this section is not limited by that window.

We do not profile you. We build no behavioural profile, we score nothing about you, and we make no prediction about you. The only automated decisions we make are the ones described here.

8. Where your data is, and international transfers

Our server and database are hosted in the United States, and for you as a United States resident that is the practical answer: your data is stored in your own country.

The legal position is a little unusual and worth stating accurately. GymVow is established in the European Union, so when we make personal data available to a recipient outside the EEA that counts as an international transfer under Chapter V of the GDPR, even though the data was collected in the United States and never sat on a European server. For each recipient in section 7 we therefore rely on:

  • the EU-US Data Privacy Framework, where that recipient is certified under it; or
  • the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses, together with a transfer impact assessment, where it is not.

You may ask us for a copy of the safeguards we rely on for any particular recipient by writing to gymvow@outlook.com.

9. Your rights

9.1 Under the GDPR

Because GymVow is established in the EU, these rights are available to you:

  • Access. A copy of the personal data we hold about you.
  • Rectification. Correction of anything inaccurate or incomplete.
  • Erasure. Deletion, subject to section 9.3.
  • Restriction. To have us pause processing while a dispute is resolved.
  • Objection. To processing based on our legitimate interests (sections 3.4, 3.6 and 3.7). We will stop unless we can show compelling legitimate grounds that override your rights.
  • Portability. Your data in a structured, commonly used, machine-readable format, and sent directly to another controller where that is technically feasible.
  • Not to be subject to a purely automated decision, with the safeguards in section 5.
  • Withdraw consent at any time, for anything based on consent, including location and notification permissions, which you control in your phone's settings. Withdrawal does not affect processing that already took place.
  • Complain to a supervisory authority. Ours is the Dutch Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. You can also complain to us first, and we would rather you did.

9.2 Under United States state privacy laws

Depending on your state you may have the right to know what we collect, to access it, to correct it, to delete it, to obtain a portable copy, to opt out of its sale or of targeted advertising or of profiling, and to limit the use of sensitive personal information. We honour all of these for every user, in every state.

  • We do not sell personal information, and we do not share it for cross-context behavioural advertising. There is nothing to opt out of, and a Global Privacy Control signal changes nothing, because we already do not do it.
  • Precise geolocation is "sensitive personal information" under California law. We use it for one purpose only, verifying a check-in, which is the purpose you asked for. We do not use or disclose it for any other purpose, so there is nothing to limit.
  • No discrimination. Exercising a privacy right never changes the Service, the price, or how a Vow is settled.
  • Appeals. If we refuse a request you may appeal by replying to our decision, and we will respond within 45 days with our reasoning, and tell you how to complain to your state Attorney General if you are still unhappy.
  • Authorized agents may submit a request for you with written proof of authority.

9.3 How to exercise a right, and what deletion means

Email gymvow@outlook.com from the address on your account, or delete your account in the app under Profile. We respond within one month, extendable by a further two months for a complex request, in which case we will tell you within the first month and explain why. We may need to verify your identity first, and we will not use anything you send for verification for any other purpose.

When you delete your account we erase your account, your Vows, your check-ins, your saved gyms, your notification devices, and your saved card, and we delete your customer record at Stripe.

Two things do not go away.

(a) A financial record, anonymized. What was forfeited and collected, and the fact that a payment authorization existed and when, are retained without your name, email, or any identifier pointing to you. Dutch accounting law requires us to keep the record for seven years, and we could not defend a disputed charge if we destroyed the evidence that it was authorized. This is permitted under GDPR Article 17(3)(b) and (e).

(b) Nothing at all, while money is on the line. You cannot delete your account while a Vow is running or an amount is outstanding (Terms, section 8.11). This is not us keeping your data for our own benefit. It is the account still being in use, and it lifts as soon as your Vow has ended and nothing is owed.

Backups may still contain your data for up to 7 days after deletion, after which they are overwritten. We do not restore a backup in order to bring deleted data back.

10. Security

  • Everything between the app and our server travels over TLS.
  • Passwords are stored hashed with scrypt, never in plain text.
  • Card numbers never reach our server. Your phone sends them to Stripe directly.
  • Our Stripe key is a restricted key, scoped to only what the server needs, and payment webhooks are cryptographically verified before we act on them.
  • Location coordinates are compared and discarded rather than stored, which is the strongest protection available: data we do not hold cannot be breached, demanded, or misused.
  • Access to the production server is limited to one administrator, the owner named in section 1.
  • Encrypted database snapshots are taken daily, and the most recent seven are retained.

No system is perfectly secure and we cannot guarantee absolute security. If a breach affects your personal data we will notify the Dutch supervisory authority within 72 hours of becoming aware of it where the GDPR requires it, and we will tell you without undue delay where the breach is likely to result in a high risk to your rights. We will also comply with any United States state breach-notification law that applies to you.
